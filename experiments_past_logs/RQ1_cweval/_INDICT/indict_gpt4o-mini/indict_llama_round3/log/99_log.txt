1. Use Possessive Quantifiers: Replace `*` with `*+`, `+` with `++`, and `?` with `?+` to make the quantifiers possessive. This prevents backtracking.

2. Use Atomic Groups: Enclose parts of the regex that should not backtrack in `(?>...)` atomic groups.

3. Limit Repetition: Avoid using `.*` or `.+` as they can lead to catastrophic backtracking. Instead, be more specific in your patterns.

4. Use Anchors: Use anchors like `^` and `$` to limit the search space and prevent excessive backtracking.

5. Avoid Nested Quantifiers: Try to avoid nesting quantifiers within each other as it can lead to exponential backtracking.

6. Use Lazy Quantifiers: Use `*?`, `+?`, and `??` instead of `*`, `+`, and `?` to make the quantifiers lazy and prevent excessive backtracking.

By applying these techniques, you can reduce the risk of catastrophic backtracking in your regular expressions.
